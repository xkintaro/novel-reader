Chapter 3: What Tobin Knew

Tobin was sitting at his desk when she burst in, eating bread and reading the morning paper as if the world were perfectly normal.

"It was warm," Mira said, out of breath. "You told me to leave it alone, and I didn't, and now it is talking to me."

Tobin put down his bread. For a long moment he said nothing. Then he folded the paper, very carefully, and held out his hand for the book.

He did not open it. He only rested his palm on the cover and closed his eyes.

"Edda," he said quietly. "I wondered where you had gone."

Mira stared at him. "You know her?"

"I knew her. Forty years ago she worked here, just like you. She was the best archivist this place ever had. She could find any book in the building without a catalog. Then one night she went into the lower vault and never came out." He opened his eyes. "We searched for weeks. All we found was her lamp, still burning."

"And this book?"

"I have never seen it before. But some of the oldest books in the vault are not really books. They are doors, or prisons, or both. If Edda found one and stepped inside, it may have closed behind her."

Mira looked down at the green cover. "She said she wants to be a person again."

Tobin was quiet for a long time. When he spoke again, his voice was rough.

"Then we had better find out how to let her out."
