Chapter 6: The Last Page

Mira tried to stop him. She tried for an hour, and then for another hour, until the candles burned low and she ran out of reasons.

"I am old," Tobin told her gently. "My eyes are failing, my knees are worse, and I have spent forty years wondering what I could have done differently. Let me do this one thing right."

At midnight, they read the grey page together. The words were in a language Mira did not know, but her tongue seemed to understand them anyway. The green book grew hot, then bright, then so bright that she had to close her eyes.

When she opened them, a woman was standing in the middle of the office.

She was young, the same age she had been when she vanished, with ink on her fingers and dust on her sleeves. She looked around the room as if she had never seen light before. Then she saw the empty chair, and the green book lying on the desk, and she understood.

"Oh, Tobin," Edda whispered. "You stubborn old fool."

She picked up the book and held it against her chest. Mira watched as a single line appeared on the first page, in familiar handwriting.

It's warm in here. Don't worry about me. Take care of the Archive.

In the weeks that followed, Edda taught Mira everything Tobin never had time to teach her. How to read the catalog. How to tell a sleeping book from a dangerous one. How to walk the lower vault without waking anything.

And every night, before closing, the two of them sat at Tobin's desk and opened the green book, and told him about their day.

He always wrote back.

THE END
